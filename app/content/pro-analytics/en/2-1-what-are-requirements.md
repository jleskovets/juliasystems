To understand what requirements actually are, let's start with the official definition:

> **Software requirements** are a set of requests/statements regarding the attributes, properties, or qualities of a system to be implemented. They are created in the process of elaborating (analysing and synthesising) a software development or modification assignment.

or, according to BABOK (the Business Analysis Body of Knowledge):

> **A requirement** is a usable representation of a need, expressed as a condition or capability that helps achieve a specific goal.

Honestly, definitions like these initially sounded like gibberish to me — impossible to memorise and even harder to reproduce coherently in a technical interview, where this question comes up at least half the time.

For myself, I have defined requirements as a description of how the system will satisfy a business need. In plain language:

> **A requirement is a description of how a system should satisfy a particular need or solve a given problem.**

It is worth paying attention to the word "solution" here, because a requirement is not the system itself — it is more like an agreement about what outcome we want from the system in order to satisfy the original need.

I do not like calling requirements a simple list of bullet points that an analyst jots down after a conversation with a client. Good requirements always involve analysing needs and choosing among possible solutions, followed by an agreement on how best to address a specific problem.

Only after all of that work — the analysis, the selection, the agreement — does that solution need to be described. And it is precisely the description of the solution and the agreements reached that we will spend quite a lot of time on, because requirements come in different forms and the way they are described can vary considerably as well.
